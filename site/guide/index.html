How to work out how many moving boxes you need

Generic bedroom guesses swing wildly between minimalists and packed homes. Measuring or presetting your actual packed volume and matching it to standard box sizes gives a transparent count you can adjust.

Step-by-step measuring walkthrough

  1. Start with a home-size preset (studio, 1-BR, 2-BR, or 3-BR) or estimate packed volume by measuring full bookshelves, closet shelves, cupboards, and storage bins.
  2. Exclude furniture, mattresses, bikes, and large appliances that move unboxed.
  3. Enter the inside dimensions of the primary moving box size you plan to buy (a US medium box is roughly 18 × 14 × 12 in to 18 × 18 × 16 in).
  4. Keep usable fill around 75% to 85% to allow for cushioning paper, irregular shapes, and headspace, plus 10% to 15% spare boxes for moving-day overflow.

What to enter in the calculator

  • Boxable inventory volume: Use a home preset above or sum measured shelves, closets, and bins.
  • Inside box length: Standard US medium box is roughly 18 × 14 × 12 in (or 18 × 18 × 16 in).
  • Inside box width: Inside width of one box.
  • Inside box height: Inside height of one box.
  • Usable fill fraction: Typically 0.75 to 0.85 to leave room for wrapping and irregular shapes.
  • Spare box allowance: Buffer for last-minute closets, cables, and fragile cushioning.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Packing heavy items like books, dishes, or tools in large boxes. Heavy loads burst tape seams and exceed safe lifting limits (~40 to 50 lb).
  • Assuming 100% of inside box volume gets filled. Padding around fragile kitchenware uses 20% to 35% of the interior space.
  • Waiting until the night before moving day to buy spare boxes, when closets and junk drawers always reveal extra items.
  • Buying only one box size. Most moves need a mix of small boxes for dense goods, medium for general items, and large for bedding and pillows.

Practical ordering checklist

  • Use small boxes (1.5 cu ft) for books, canned goods, records, and tools.
  • Use medium boxes (3.0 cu ft) for kitchenware, toys, small appliances, and pantry items.
  • Reserve large boxes (4.5+ cu ft) strictly for lightweight bulky items like linens, pillows, and coats.
